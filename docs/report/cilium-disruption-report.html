Lab report · 2026-09-24/25 · Cilium 1.19.1 (upgrade target 1.19.8)

How much does Cilium itself disrupt the data plane?

A 3-node leaf-spine lab mirroring the production BGP setup (90 s hold, no BFD, no graceful restart) was put through agent rollouts, upgrades, agent OOM kills and backend OOM kills, with Maglev on and off, SNAT and DSR, and externalTrafficPolicy Cluster and Local. 148 measured runs. The short answer: almost all the damage is routing, not forwarding, and one Cilium-side setting removes it.

1

A backend pod dying costs nothing beyond its own flows. Killing an echo backend produced zero collateral breakage in every combination of algorithm, forwarding mode and traffic policy. New-connection failures stayed under 3%.

2

Almost all Cilium-caused disruption is BGP, not the BPF datapath. Without graceful restart, any agent restart closes the bird↔Cilium session and bird withdraws that node's pod CIDR and every VIP only Cilium advertises. The datapath keeps forwarding; the fabric stops sending it traffic, or stops routing replies to it.

3

A rollout or upgrade is far worse than an OOM kill. A SIGKILL restarts the agent container in place: median route gap 9 s. A rollout replaces the pod, init containers and all: median 44–47 s per node, up to 86 s, and the chart's default maxUnavailable: 2 takes two nodes out together.

4

With eTP=Cluster, ~70% of external flows stall past 30 s during a rollout, whatever the algorithm or forwarding mode, and 28–50% of new connections fail; in-cluster flows fare the same (~65%). With eTP=Local and a stable VIP route, external breakage drops to ~1%.

5

East-west traffic (pod → ClusterIP) is hit as hard as north-south, and only graceful restart fixes it. A ClusterIP flow is load-balanced once, at the client pod's own node, and the Service IP never enters the fabric, so Maglev, DSR/SNAT and traffic policy have no effect on it. What hits it is the pod-CIDR withdrawal: every flow with an endpoint on the restarting node freezes for that node's restart. 63–68% of in-cluster flows during a rollout, 64–71% stalled during an agent OOM, and 0% with graceful restart on.

6

A VIP advertised only by Cilium loses 100% of its flows in every rollout. It is withdrawn from each restarting node, so its flows land on a node with no state for them. eTP=Local does not help here; it guarantees the re-homed flow breaks.

7

A re-homed flow survives only with DSR + Maglev (pcap-attributed: 140 of 140 survived, 0 changed backend). Under SNAT every re-homed flow with a live backend broke, Maglev or not (145 of 145). Under DSR + random the new node picks a different backend 5 times in 6; a wrong local backend resets the flow, a wrong remote backend's RST leaves with the pod IP instead of the VIP and the client ignores it, so the flow stalls.

8

Enabling graceful restart on Cilium's BGP peer, and nothing else, removes nearly all of it. bird and FRR already act as helpers by default. Rollout, upgrade and OOM kill all went to 0% broken and 0% new-connection failures with no route gap, and 10 of 10 single-node pod replacements moved nothing. The residual is the End-of-RIB race below: on 1.19.1 the Cilium-only VIP was withdrawn in 2 of 31 agent restarts with GR on, for 0.7 s and 5.8 s; on 1.19.8, 0 of 29.

9

maxUnavailable: 1 halves the peak, not the total. Rolling one node at a time cut the worst new-connection failure rate from ~50% to ~27% and never had two nodes' routes missing at once, but every cross-node flow still stalls for one full agent restart.

10

Agent restart time is the whole game, and it varies. Two identical OOM kills gave a 5.7 s and a 28.7 s route gap; the first broke 0% of static-VIP flows, the second 23.5%, purely from landing either side of the 30 s timeout. A stall also outlasts the route gap by up to one TCP retransmit backoff.

11

On 1.19.1 the new agent can send End-of-RIB before its Service routes, and the GR helper then flushes the VIP. Packet captures of the bird↔Cilium session show the agent opening, announcing only the pod CIDR, sending End-of-RIB, and announcing the Service VIPs with its soft resets a moment later. bird finishes graceful restart at the marker (RFC 4724 §4.2), deletes the stale VIP and withdraws it from the leaves; the VIP comes back when the announce arrives. 1.19.1 logs 4 "Neighbor soft reset out" per agent start with 3 advertised Services and 24 with 23; 1.19.8 logs 2 either way, 1.3–1.8 s before the session opens, and never sent End-of-RIB first in 29 captured restarts. The 1.19.5 change that batches route-policy reconciliation into fewer soft resets (#45927) matches that difference.

12

More Services widen the race and add a small in-cluster reset rate. With 20 extra ClusterIP Services the VIP arrived 5.8 s after End-of-RIB on one node (0.7 s with three Services), and half the external flows re-hashed off that node. GR-off rollouts broke 82–84% of in-cluster flows (63–68% with one Service). With GR on, every 20-Service run also lost 1–6% of in-cluster flows to resets, on both versions, spread over Services, backends and time; single-Service runs never did. That is not the BGP mechanism and is not attributed here.

Setup

Topology

client → SONiC ToR → 3 FRR spines → 2 FRR leaves → 3 nodes (k3s + Cilium 1.19.1, kube-proxy replacement, native routing), each with a host bird peering to both leaves and to its local Cilium agent. 6 echo backends, spread 2 per node. Every node is dual-homed, so the fabric itself never loses a path in these tests.

BGP, mirroring production

Hold 90 s / keepalive 30 s on node↔leaf and bird↔Cilium; no BFD; no graceful restart configured anywhere. Defaults therefore apply: bird "graceful restart aware" (helper), FRR helper, Cilium GR disabled. Verified live before every batch:

Three client populations per run

      
ext-cil: external client → 192.0.2.20, a VIP advertised only by Cilium BGP. ext-static: external client → 192.0.2.21, the same Service also statically originated by bird: the "BGP unharmed" control. int: a pod on the disrupted node → the ClusterIP, with internalTrafficPolicy left at its default (Cluster) throughout. externalTrafficPolicy affects only the two external populations.

      
Each population holds 200 long-lived TCP flows (100 ms application keepalive) and runs a 10 Hz new-connection prober. The mechanism

  
When the agent process exits, its BGP session to the local bird closes. Without graceful restart (RFC 4724) bird treats that as a normal session teardown and withdraws every route the agent had announced: the node's pod CIDR and any VIP that only Cilium advertises. Those withdrawals propagate to the leaves and spines within a second. The BPF programs on the node keep running the whole time.

  
Route presence at leaf1 during three agent rollouts
Each lane: seconds (from the restart command) during which that node's pod CIDR was absent from leaf1, and the number of nexthops for the Cilium-only VIP. Top: chart default (two nodes at a time). Middle: maxUnavailable: 1. Bottom: graceful restart on.

  
Two consequences follow. A flow whose backend sits on the restarting node has nowhere to be forwarded to; its packets are dropped, as a real fabric would drop them. And a flow that had entered through that node for a Cilium-only VIP is moved by the leaf's ECMP to another node, which has no state for it.

  
How long a node's routes stay withdrawn
Every per-node pod-CIDR gap observed in the prod-mirror runs, by disruption type. The 30 s line is the flow timeout used to call a stall "broken".

  
The gap is exactly the agent's restart time. An OOM kill restarts one container in place (median 9 s here). A rollout or upgrade deletes the pod and starts a new one through all six init containers (median 44–47 s here, inflated by this lab's slow storage). Production will be faster, but the shape is the same: routes are gone for the whole restart.

East-west traffic: pod → ClusterIP

  
Every run also measured a client pod holding 200 connections to the Service's ClusterIP, with a 10 Hz new-connection prober. In the main matrix that pod sat on the disrupted node, the worst case; a follow-up put it on a healthy node. internalTrafficPolicy stayed at its default (Cluster) throughout.

  
None of the load-balancing knobs apply here. The client's own node picks the backend once and keeps it in conntrack, the ClusterIP never appears in the fabric, and there is no second node to re-home to: across all sixteen eTP=Cluster rollout and upgrade cells the in-cluster result sat in 63–68% whatever the algorithm or forwarding mode. The one thing that reaches a ClusterIP flow is the pod-CIDR withdrawal. When a node's agent restarts without GR, every flow with an endpoint on that node, either end, has no route for its packets or its replies until the agent re-peers.

The fix for east-west is graceful restart, and nothing else helps. Traffic policy, Maglev and DSR are north-south tools. For pod-to-Service traffic the only variable is whether the node's pod CIDR stays in the fabric while the agent restarts, and GR is what keeps it there. With 25,000 pods, each restarting node without GR freezes every east-west flow touching its pods for the length of its restart, and maxUnavailable: k freezes k nodes' worth at once.

Why each outcome happens

  
SNAT. The new ingress node masquerades with its own IP, so the backend sees a new 4-tuple mid-stream and resets it (RFC 9293 §3.5.2). Maglev choosing the same backend changes nothing. Captured on node1 during an agent kill (static VIP, SNAT):

  

  
DSR + Maglev. The new node forwards to the same backend with the client's real source address; the backend node already holds the DSR conntrack entry from the original SYN, so the packet is delivered and the pod continues. 140 of 140 survived across two runs.

  
DSR + random. The new node picks another backend. If that backend is local to the new node, the pod gets the segment and replies with an RST that is reverse-translated to the VIP and reaches the client (17 and 18 resets in the two runs, 27% of re-homed). If it is remote, the pod still replies with an RST, but the backend node has no DSR state for that flow and sends it out with the pod's own IP as source; the client, connected to the VIP, discards it, and the flow stalls. Verified with a capture of every RST on every node during a DSR + random kill:

  

  
RSTs after the kill, counted per (interface, direction, src → dst). Pods on node1 and node3 emit RSTs to the client (lxc… In), and the nodes forward them out the fabric with the pod IP as source. Only a handful leave as 192.0.2.20 > client. Hubble on those nodes showed no drops other than FIB_LOOKUP_FAILED toward node2's pods, the withdrawn CIDR.

  

  
One such flow, seen in the 1-second reduction of the capture (client source port 35777, random + DSR). Before the kill it enters node2 and is forwarded to a node1 pod; at +1 s it enters node1, which forwards it to a node3 pod; the packet rate collapses to retransmissions; at +8 s node2's route returns, the flow comes home and resumes:

  

  
Every re-home in this lab was transient: node2's route came back after ~10 s and most flows moved back to their original node, where their state still lived. That is why "stalled" flows survived. In production a re-home is usually permanent (the node is gone), so those flows would stall until the client gave up. A permanently re-homed flow survives only under DSR + Maglev. SNAT breaks it by RST; DSR + random by RST or by an RST the client never accepts.

End-of-RIB before the Service routes

  
With graceful restart, the helper keeps the restarting peer's routes as stale and deletes whatever has not been re-advertised when the End-of-RIB marker arrives (RFC 4724 §4.2). So the order of the new agent's first messages decides whether a VIP survives the restart: if the marker goes out before the Service advertisement, the helper flushes the VIP and the fabric sees a withdrawal until the advertisement lands.

  
To check that, every run in these batches captured the bird↔Cilium session on each node (tcp/179, decoded with tcpdump -vv), bird's log with the cilium protocol traced, and the restarted agent's log. The one captured 1.19.1 delete that moved flows shows the sequence directly (node2, seconds from the pod delete; the bird → leaf line is what the ToR saw):

  

  
The agent opens, announces its pod CIDR and sends End-of-RIB at +28.26 s. Its service reconciler runs 0.7 s later: three more pod-CIDR + End-of-RIB pairs (one per soft reset) and then the three VIPs. bird's log in between: Got END-OF-RIB, Neighbor graceful restart done, the stale 192.0.2.20 deleted and withdrawn to both leaves at +28.36 s, re-announced at +29.02 s. 79 of 200 external flows re-hashed off node2 in that 0.65 s and every one broke under SNAT. The 0.25 s leaf sampler fell in a gap and missed it; the uplink capture is the evidence.

  

  
bird's log on node2 for the same restart (debug all on the cilium protocol).

  
With 20 extra Services the reconciler's first pass takes longer, and the window grows with it. In the first GR-on rollout at 20 Services, node3's agent sent End-of-RIB 5.8 s before its VIPs:

Cilium 1.19.8 agents log 2 soft resets per start instead of 4 (or 24 with 23 advertised prefixes), both 1.3–1.8 s before the session opens, and put every prefix in the first UPDATE before End-of-RIB in all 29 captured restarts, including the three captured upgrades from 1.19.1 and the four single-node replacements with the ClusterIPs advertised. The 1.19.5 release notes carry the matching change, "Reduce amount of soft peer resets by service reconciliation" (#45927), which batches the route-policy reconciliation that 1.19.3's service-advertisement fix (#45049) had made per-Service.

  
What this means for production. Graceful restart removes the restart gap, and on 1.19.1 it leaves a race whose window is the service reconciler's first pass: under a second with a handful of Services, several seconds with 23, and longer with the Service count of a real cluster. Running 1.19.5 or later closes it in every capture here. Originating VIPs independently of the agent, as the bird-static .21 does, covers it on any version.

Recommended changes

  
1
Enable graceful restart on Cilium's BGP peer config
Set gracefulRestart.enabled: true with restartTimeSeconds longer than the slowest agent pod start (120 s covered 86 s here). bird's default is already "aware" and FRR's is helper, so this is a Cilium-side change only; it needs no GR or BFD on the fabric. It took every agent disruption in this lab from up to 100% broken to 0%, for north-south and east-west alike; for pod-to-Service traffic it is the only change that helps. Pair it with Cilium 1.19.5 or later: on 1.19.1 the new agent can send End-of-RIB before its Service routes and the helper flushes the VIP for the length of the reconciler's first pass (0.7 s with three Services, 5.8 s with 23); 1.19.8 never did in 29 captured restarts.
Reference: RFC 4724 §4.2; Cilium BGP Control Plane docs (Graceful Restart); Cilium v1.19.5 release notes, #45927. Also originate VIPs independently of the agent (bird static, as .21 here) to cover the case where an agent is down longer than the restart timer.

  
2
Use eTP=Local only where the VIP route is stable, and keep ≥2 ready backends per node
eTP=Local removes the cross-node dependency that caused ~70% external breakage during rollouts. But if a VIP can be withdrawn from a node, eTP=Local guarantees those flows break. Pod anti-affinity or topology spread that keeps two backends per node covers the last-backend case.

  
3
Roll the Cilium DaemonSet with updateStrategy.rollingUpdate.maxUnavailable: 1 and pre-pull images
Halves the peak failure rate and keeps only one node's routes missing at a time. Without GR every cross-node flow still stalls for one agent restart; treat this as damage limitation, not a fix.

  
4
Where flows can re-home, run DSR + Maglev
The only combination in which a re-homed flow survived (140/140, pcap-verified). Maglev without DSR bought nothing in any test: under SNAT every re-homed flow with a live backend broke. This resolves the open SNAT question from the earlier Maglev work.

  
5
Backend OOMs need no Cilium-side mitigation
Zero collateral in every configuration. The one exception is the eTP=Local last-backend case in recommendation 2.

  
6
Two things worth raising upstream
(a) Under DSR, an RST from a pod for a flow the backend node has no DSR state for leaves with the pod's IP as source and can never reach the client's socket; a fast failure becomes a stall. (b) With many Services, 1–6% of in-cluster flows reset during an agent restart with GR on, on 1.19.1 and 1.19.8 alike, while the BGP side is clean; cause not identified here.


  
Caveats

  
    
    
  • Absolute gap durations are lab-inflated. Agent pod start-up on WSL over 9p storage is slow. The mechanism (routes absent for the agent's whole restart) is not lab-specific.
    • 
    
  • 3 nodes with maxUnavailable: 2 means two thirds of the cluster had routes withdrawn at once. In a large cluster the fraction hit per step is maxUnavailable / N, but each affected flow sees the same gap.
    • 
    
  • "Broken" during rollouts means stalled past 30 s. Real TCP retransmits for minutes (the eCDF above shows the distribution); most application timeouts would not.
    • 
    
  • Every re-home here was transient (node2's route returned after ~10 s). Survival figures for anything other than DSR + Maglev depend on that.
    • 
    
  • The prod-mirror matrix ran without per-flow capture. Its agent-kill cells were re-run with capture and agree with it; the rollout cells' "100% ext-cil" is by construction, since every node restarts.
    • 
    
  • The GR comparison is one configuration, 2 runs each, plus 10 single-node replacements. Enough to show the route gap disappears; the re-peer race needs its own reproduction.
    • 
    
  • One target node (node2) for kills; 2 runs per cell. Between-run spread was small except where restart time crossed the 30 s threshold.
